\section{\MakeUppercase{2. RankBind}}

\lead{Frozen ChemBERTa (384-d) and ESM2-650M (1280-d) encoders, 256-d
projectors, \hlb{627,201} trainable parameters. Four ingredients, ordered by
how much each contributes:}

{\small
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{3mm}
	\item \textbf{Within-ligand margin loss} (\textit{k} = 4, \textit{m} = 1.0), computed inside
	each molecule's own candidate set, so the model optimises a ranking rather
	than a per-pair binary decision. {\color{scadsaigrey}Remove it: matrix MRR
	0.326\,$\rightarrow$\,0.041, while pooled AUC rebounds to 0.95 and the shortcut
	returns at once.}
	\item \textbf{Online hard-negative mining}: at each epoch start the
	positive-molecule\,$\times$\,train-protein score matrix is refreshed, and the
	\textit{k} negatives are drawn from the 50 highest-scoring non-binding proteins
	per anchor. {\color{scadsaigrey}Adding it: 0.201\,$\rightarrow$\,0.326
	(+62\%).}
	\item \textbf{Protein-balanced sampler}: equal positives and negatives per
	protein per epoch, or frequent proteins dominate the batches and re-imprint
	the popularity prior. {\color{scadsaigrey}Remove it: $-$44\% MRR.}
	\item \textbf{Low-rank bilinear head} (rank 128) instead of MLP-concat at
	matched capacity. {\color{scadsaigrey}Same mean, 2.2$\times$ tighter seed
	spread (s.d.\ 0.072 vs 0.161).}
\end{enumerate}}

\panel{\panelhead{Residue-level extension}
{\small A learned-query attention pool over per-residue ESM2 embeddings
(\hlb{+3,840} parameters, +0.6\%) lifts matrix MRR
\hlb{0.326\,$\rightarrow$\,0.427} and Hit@10 to \hlb{0.814}. The audit says the
gain is \emph{not} pocket selection: catalytic residues sit at the 3rd
attention percentile of their own protein. The mechanism is per-residue
LayerNorm before pooling, so a parameter-free LayerNorm baseline is the cheap
first stop.}}
